## W3C public working-group archive

### Verified routes and manageable sample

The [W3C public archive directory](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/) lists [the `w3c-wai-gl` archive](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-gl/), identified on its cover as the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines Working Group discussion list. The [quarter page sorted by date](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-gl/2026JulSep/) reports 184 messages from July 3 to September 10, 2026. The [thread-sorted view](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-gl/2026JulSep/thread.html) reports 150. The archive cover page displayed 192 for this quarter in a separate, more recently crawled copy. These are source-page counters, not independently recomputed counts; retain the discrepancy in provenance rather than choosing an unexplained number.

Individual HTML messages are accessible directly and are a practical small-sample route. Four verified candidate pages cover several coordination patterns:

- [0094.html](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-gl/2026JulSep/0094.html): explicit review/action with a due date; the page links three replies.
- [0096.html](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-gl/2026JulSep/0096.html): meeting agenda with date/time and agenda items.
- [0124.html](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-gl/2026JulSep/0124.html): call-for-consensus parent with a deadline and many linked replies.
- [0178.html](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-gl/2026JulSep/0178.html): consensus outcome linked back to the call.

For an initial parser/rights-review exercise, a manually selected 8–20-message sample can follow reply links from those pages and retain only needed HTML fields, source URL, and source identifiers in a controlled local directory. Do not infer that this limited sample is training-approved. The thread view links messages across the period and is preferable to inferring parentage from `Re:` subjects. The quarter index and individual pages are stable-looking historical URLs suitable for a reproducible sample; recheck availability at acquisition time.

### Availability, raw-mail formats, and metadata

The archive cover’s “messages in mailbox format” link resolves to `https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-gl/mboxes/`. Following it redirected to W3C authentication (`auth.w3.org`); no anonymous month mbox or raw RFC822 endpoint was verified. Direct per-message `.html` pages do work. Do not build a raw-mail adapter around an assumed `.mbox`, `.mbx`, or `.eml` suffix.

The [W3C archive help](https://lists.w3.org/Help/) describes per-message pages as including subject, sender, date, recipient, and Message-ID, with links to the prior message, next in thread, and replies when available. On the sampled page, the stable visible markers include `# <subject>`, `This message`, `Related messages`, `From:`, `Date:`, `To:`, and `Message-ID:`; a `Received on` line and Hypermail footer follow the body. Reply pages can expose `In reply to`; thread navigation is link-based, not a promise of complete RFC822 `In-Reply-To`/`References` or MIME headers. Body text may contain quoted history, HTML links, signatures, and forwarded material, so a page parser should keep the current message body distinct from navigation and quoted text. Avoid capturing unrelated linked pages.

### Coordination value, noise, and privacy

The selected WAI list contains agendas, invitations to review work by a stated date, calls for consensus, discussion/replies, and published outcomes. That provides genuine working-group coordination examples and reply context, but it is standards-group work rather than university project email. Noise includes accessibility/standards terminology, technical document references, survey and issue links, recurring meeting notices, external warning banners, long quoted chains, and system/status messages. A date in a standards discussion is not automatically an FYP deadline; label only the current author’s operational intent.

The pages reveal names, email addresses, employer/contact signatures, and potentially personal details. A public archive may also include a link or reference to a meeting resource that the message describes as nonpublic. Human-review the candidate pages for unnecessary personal data and confidential meeting information before preserving even a local copy; do not publish message bodies or personal headers in Git.

### Rights distinction

W3C’s [mailing-list page](https://www.w3.org/email/) says first-time posters to public-archive lists must give W3C permission to publish their message in the public archive. W3C’s [archive-use guidance](https://www.w3.org/Mail/W3CArchiveUsage.html) describes public posts as archived and generally permanent. This documents the publisher’s right to host the public archive; I found no explicit license in these pages that grants this project bulk copying, corpus redistribution, or ML-training rights in the user-authored email text.

The separate [W3C intellectual-rights page](https://www.w3.org/copyright/intellectual-rights/) scopes the W3C document license to W3C documents and says original document authors retain copyright. That publisher-site/document policy should not be silently applied to list authors’ email bodies. Conversely, the absence of a corpus license in the pages reviewed is not a finding that limited academic reading is prohibited. I found no express prohibition of a bounded internal candidate-review sample, but no general reuse grant either; treat retention as a cautious review step pending university/legal review, not as a claim of entitlement. Before training or public reproduction, seek a rights determination/permission as applicable. This distinction is also why “publicly readable” is not recorded as a data license.

## EmailSum W3C code and raw-data route

The archived [EmailSum repository](https://github.com/ZhangShiyue/EmailSum) README says it “only release[s] the summaries” and provides scripts to extract threads from flat Avocado or W3C email corpora. For W3C it instructs users to put raw data files in `W3C/raw_data/`, run `extract_threads.py`, then `anonymize.py` to produce `W3C_threads/`; it points to a [separate Google Drive folder](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1ZPGdzvauoEN4qqsZ2ZxD4EkWobCZPZhZ?usp=sharing). I did not open or download that folder. The README gives no W3C raw-message count, source-list inventory, provenance chain, or separate data license for those messages.

EmailSum’s README reports **2,549 Avocado summary threads**; that number is not a W3C email or W3C thread count. The repository has a root [MIT license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZhangShiyue/EmailSum/main/LICENSE), naming Microsoft Corporation and UNC NLP and licensing “Software” and associated documentation. It licenses the repository software under that notice, but it is not evidence that those authors could license third-party W3C messages, or that LDC Avocado text or EmailSum summaries inherit the same terms. The repo is archived read-only since March 2026. Recommendation: reuse the publicly licensed code only as a parser reference, if useful; skip the Drive raw-email route unless the data publisher/rights holder and reuse conditions are independently established.

## RADAR Action-Item: existing acquisition, no redo

The repository’s [external acquisition report](external_dataset_acquisition.md) and [RADAR integration report](radar_action_integration.md) already document the [official CMU release](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~pbennett/action-item-dataset.html) and local acquisition. They report 744 messages, 328 positive and 416 negative action-presence judgments, and 416 action spans. CMU describes same-length random token substitution and says message threading, From/To/Date, and all headers except subject were removed. The existing audit validated message/span files; this review did not download or re-audit them.

The CMU page describes research distribution after IRB review but does not name an open license or grant general redistribution. Treat this as an auxiliary source for its original action-presence/span task; it has poor lexical transfer because tokens are replaced and weak thread/coordination value because headers/threading are absent. Do not map all positive examples to FYP `ACTION_REQUEST`: the source “action item” task is not equivalent to a current author request.

## Avocado / LDC2015T03: access is institution-dependent

The current [LDC catalog entry](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2015T03) lists Web Download and requires both the [organizational](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/license/Avocado%20Collection%20-%20Organization%20Agreement.pdf) and [individual end-user agreements](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/license/Avocado%20Collection%20-%20Individual%20Agreement.pdf). The existing [Avocado access report](avocado_acquisition.md) records no login, purchase, or executed agreement, and says Bahria entitlement and the product-specific fee are unknown; the catalog exposes the applicable fee after login. A public 2026 membership listing would not prove access to this product’s 2015 membership year.

The official [README v1.0.3](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/docs/LDC2015T03/README.txt) reports 2,033,740 total items, 938,035 email items, and 614,461 nonduplicate email texts. It documents useful IDs, reconstructed or retained message headers, and `in_reply_to` links for some emails; its deduplication is explicitly approximate, and Exchange headers may be reconstructed. PII/sensitivity handling remains material: the README reports 235 “company confidential,” 10 “personal,” and 67 “private” email sensitivity labels, with personal/private messages redacted in the release.

The individual agreement says the collection may contain sensitive PII, prohibits redistribution or giving any other person access, prohibits public reproduction of any part (even small excerpts), and requires organizational human-subjects training/approval. The organizational agreement confines access to authenticated users inside the institution who each signed the end-user agreement, and prohibits publishing, transmitting, redistributing, or otherwise reproducing the collection. Therefore this is a potentially valuable thread/context source only after a legitimate LDC account-level fee/entitlement check, authorized signatures, and institutional approval. Do not substitute EmailSum’s public repo or Drive link for the official LDC route.

## Additional credible source: IETF HTTP Working Group archive

The IETF’s [open-records page](https://www.ietf.org/about/open-records/) documents permanent per-message public archive URLs, an rsync archive (`rsync.ietf.org::mailman-archive`), and IMAP access. The [IETF mailing-list page](https://www.ietf.org/participate/lists/) says working-group archives can be searched/read and describes importable `.eml` downloads, rsync, and anonymous IMAP at `imap.ietf.org:993` (username `anonymous`, email address as password). I did not use IMAP, rsync, or download an export. On the tested Mail Archive search view, the bulk archive-export controls said login was required, so use public per-message URLs for a bounded sample and do not assume export is anonymous.

The [active `httpbis` Datatracker page](https://datatracker.ietf.org/group/httpbis/about/) identifies the HTTP Working Group and its list address `ietf-http-wg@w3.org`; the archive search name is `httpbisa`. An archive view displayed about 40,000 messages (40,038 on one captured view); this is a drifting UI total, not a locally parsed count. A manageable exact sample route is the [August 2026 `httpbisa` archive view](https://mailarchive.ietf.org/arch/browse/httpbisa/?index=yGkDJc7AyYjPr65KEN5RK4bXCSM) and the linked [call-for-adoption parent](https://mailarchive.ietf.org/arch/msg/httpbisa/eAYwuU5JQQAPirjFuuVYErBHcyo/), with example replies [`dWmOaYVPZcBNvdoIpk4pIGH5GwE`](https://mailarchive.ietf.org/arch/msg/httpbisa/dWmOaYVPZcBNvdoIpk4pIGH5GwE/), [`A3x3QcLBZJ7NwFXlmU2YuJOH4YI`](https://mailarchive.ietf.org/arch/msg/httpbisa/A3x3QcLBZJ7NwFXlmU2YuJOH4YI/), and [`zELr94bksAFrtZCRPmRxJvtGMVc`](https://mailarchive.ietf.org/arch/msg/httpbisa/zELr94bksAFrtZCRPmRxJvtGMVc/). These are a compact call, support/opposition, and follow-up slice; select only the required pages, not the full archive.

IETF pages expose useful coordination fields and reply context: a verified call page includes From/To, Date, Subject, Message-ID, `Archived-At`, and list-resend fields; replies commonly quote prior messages. This supports calls for adoption, support/objection, comment deadlines, and follow-up. Noise includes automatic IETF Secretariat/RFC Editor notices, technical reviews, dense specification jargon, quoted history, and duplicated quoted text. The domain is less like FYP team mail than its coordination mechanics are like a working group.

IETF reuse terms are more explicit than the W3C archive pages, but are not a generic dataset license. The IETF Trust FAQ says an email to an IETF list in the activity context is an IETF Contribution; the author retains copyright but grants the Trust rights under RFC 5378. The FAQ says the Trust lets anyone publish and translate unmodified Contributions outside the standards process, while other external derivative-work rights are not granted except case-by-case. Treat model-training transformations and release of derived samples as unresolved for this project; do not infer that a public archive or a Trust license makes the messages an open ML corpus. The [IETF Note Well](https://www.ietf.org/about/note-well/) explicitly warns that public activity records include participant personal information. Use only a human-reviewed, access-controlled sample until reuse and privacy review is complete.
