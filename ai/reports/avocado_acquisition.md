# Avocado access investigation

Reviewed 2026-10-02 from public LDC and university sources. No LDC login,
account registration, purchase, agreement, form submission, or communication
was made.

## Current official route

The [current LDC2015T03 catalog entry](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2015T03)
still lists the Avocado Research Email Collection as a Web Download. The entry
shows `Member Year(s): 2015`, offers Login/Register, identifies the licensing
route as “Subscription & Standard Members, and Non-Members,” and requires both
the [Avocado Organizational License Agreement](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/license/Avocado%20Collection%20-%20Organization%20Agreement.pdf)
and the [Avocado End User Agreement](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/license/Avocado%20Collection%20-%20Individual%20Agreement.pdf).
The [official README v1.0.3](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/docs/LDC2015T03/README.txt)
also instructs every user to sign the End User Agreement before working with
the collection.

The product page links to LDC’s general data-obtaining instructions for
subscription/standard members and non-members. That instructions page was not
exposed by the public crawler used for this review. I rely only on the route
names shown in the product catalog and do not infer further account workflow
details.

LDC’s [2026 not-for-profit membership page](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2026MNP)
says universities may hold non-profit membership for non-commercial linguistic
education/research, with standard membership benefits tied to data released in
the membership year and possible extra charges for specified datasets.
Avocado’s catalog member year is 2015. A current 2026 membership listing does
not establish that Bahria has membership, that it includes this 2015 dataset,
or that the Avocado-specific agreements have been completed. Confirm any
account- or dataset-specific entitlement with the authorized university LDC
contact or LDC before relying on it.

## Agreement and data-handling implications

The Organizational License Agreement limits use to linguistic research and
technology development. It requires stored data access to be controlled and
limited to authenticated people within the organization who have each signed
the End User Agreement. The organization retains those signed agreements.
The organization agreement itself requires two signatures: an officer
authorized to make legal commitments for the organization and the person
responsible for using the collection. Each end user must sign separately before
being given access.

The End User Agreement prohibits redistribution and public reproduction of
corpus material, requires whatever organizational training and approval applies
to human-subjects data, and limits any Internet-accessible derived work to
authenticated, signed users within the organization. It also requires the
collection and company to be referred to only as “Avocado” and not to speculate
about the company’s identity. The signed agreements control the exact terms.
